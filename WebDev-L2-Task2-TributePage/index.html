A life of purpose

From a coastal town to the heart of a nation

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, in 1931. His childhood was modest, shaped by a close-knit island community and a family that valued honesty, discipline, and service. Those early years did not promise an easy path, but they gave him the curiosity and determination that would guide the rest of his life.

After studying physics and aeronautical engineering, Kalam entered India's growing scientific institutions. At ISRO, he led the project that developed SLV-III, India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle. Its successful launch of the Rohini satellite in 1980 marked an important step in the country's ability to reach space using its own technology.

Kalam later brought teams and institutions together in India's guided missile programme. His work on projects including Agni and Prithvi earned him the popular title “Missile Man of India.” Yet he rarely presented achievement as the work of one person. He spoke instead about shared missions, courageous teams, and the lessons hidden inside failure.

When he became India's eleventh President in 2002, he carried the spirit of a teacher into public office. He used the presidency to speak directly with students and encourage them to connect knowledge with national progress. Long after leaving office, he continued teaching, writing, and meeting young people—remaining devoted to education until his final day.

Milestones

A journey measured in service

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Grew up in the island town of Rameswaram in Tamil Nadu.

  2. 1960

    Began an aerospace career

    Completed aeronautical engineering and joined India's defence research establishment.

  3. 1980

    SLV-III reached orbit

    Led the project that placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit.

  4. 1997

    Received the Bharat Ratna

    Was awarded India's highest civilian honour for his national service.

  5. 2002

    Became President of India

    Started a five-year term as the country's eleventh President.

  6. 2015

    A teacher to the end

    Passed away while delivering a lecture to students in Shillong.

Why he matters

A legacy larger than any office

Kalam made science feel connected to everyday hope. He showed that technical ambition could exist alongside humility, poetry, and a deep concern for people. His example continues to invite students to treat learning not only as a route to employment, but as a way to solve meaningful problems.

This tribute remembers him not simply for rockets, awards, or the presidency, but for his faith in young minds. His most enduring project may be the confidence he placed in generations he would never live to see grow old.
